Figure and table descriptions

These descriptions use the same text as the manuscript’s LaTeX Description commands. Figures and tables retain their own numbering.

Figures

  1. Three panels show the research questions, evidence, and comparisons. The questions concern representations, workflow, and external cognition. Prior knowledge informs each comparison. Two separately counted review streams record interface mechanisms and knowledge claims. The final panel connects features to activities, distinguishes what tools provide from what people practice, and asks which task, conditions, and outcomes an earlier study tested. These comparisons were developed with language-model assistance and await author review.
  2. Stream 1 screens 35,480 titles and abstracts and selects 7,372 papers for full-text reading. Round 2 examines a separate 3,357-paper subset. Stream 2 combines three overlapping discovery routes and records 543 cards with 3,017 claims. Source identity holds are counted separately. Both streams retain source pages.
  3. Stacked bars count selected papers by publication year, with seven numbered disciplinary venue groups. Year totals for 2019 through 2026 are 600, 692, 756, 776, 865, 987, 1,457, and 1,239. Human-computer interaction is the largest group in every year. The 2026 bar is partial because the cutoff was 16 July; missing proceedings explain zero selections for some groups. Below the chart, all forty venues have selection counts and shares. These are selected review records, not annual publication totals for the fields.
  4. Two parallel lanes. Stream 1 has two reading rounds with two criteria each: a designed structure and task meaning, followed by a person-facing representation and consequential use. Stream 2 checks for a relevant, transferable claim supported by a specific passage. The outcomes distinguish inclusion, context only, and exclusion.
  5. The separate Stream 1 and Stream 2 paper identities feed a metadata matching step, which branches to reference lists, citing papers, and citation counts. Retrieval date and missing, empty, or truncated responses are retained.
  6. Three paths connect representations, workflow, and external cognition to their source fields and comparisons. Prior-work passages and Stream 2 claims inform all three paths; source populations remain separate.
  7. Four stages connect source evidence, evidence cards, shared vocabulary, and cross-case interpretation. Automated record checks and selective author review are shown separately, with no universal human approval gate.
  8. Three line-chart panels track cumulative vocabulary during reading, separately for Stream 1 Round 1, Stream 1 Round 2, and Stream 2. The horizontal axis is cards read and the vertical axis is the percentage of each recorded final vocabulary encountered. Grouped codes level off earlier than the original wording and cited-work vocabularies. Curves end at the observed coverage of each coding field; an ending is not evidence that unread papers would add no codes.
  9. A hierarchical profile groups research activities by application domain, with paper counts and shares for 2,972 eligible papers with domain codes. One paper may contribute to more than one domain. The grouping describes what the reviewed systems support, separately from the disciplinary venue groups in the method figure.
  10. Five families and eighteen categories, each with a count and share of 2,412 papers. Organisation and comparison and guidance and feedback each appear in about 72 percent, explanation and visibility in 62.5 percent, user control in 50.7 percent, and collaboration in 13.3 percent.
  11. Five groups of three horizontal bars compare 533 papers using conventional computation, 1,136 using AI without LLMs, and 704 using LLMs. User control occurs in 45.4, 49.2, and 58.0 percent, respectively.
  12. Four overlapping circles show specifying intent, representing possibilities, evaluating alternatives, and acting and revising. A return arrow connects activities, above a shared layer of memory and coordination through annotations, histories and shared views.
  13. Connected groups from 106,521 cited sources, arranged by co-citation neighbourhoods. There are 157 stem sources, 5,591 branch sources and 100,773 leaves. Seven large neighbourhoods are labelled. Sources without group connections remain available in the online source list.
  14. Three types branch into knowledge categories. Design knowledge accounts for 62.5% of classified uses, computational methods for 21.0%, and cognition and human activity for 16.5%. Each category has a count and percentage of the 4,126 classified uses.
  15. Six families of workflow change with counts and shares. Work changes form in 92.3 percent of 2,752 papers, and a step becomes automatic in 26.3 percent. Less frequent families include newly possible, additional, and unchanged work.
  16. Two parallel profiles list families of delegated and retained work with counts and shares. Computation and judgement is the largest tool family; judgement and decision is the largest human family.
  17. Three rows connect supplied material to human activity and assessment. Learners create subgoal labels, practice with their own, expert or no labels, then solve problems without labels. ExPeerience supplies AI-assisted contexts and tables while learners write and check SQL; the study records process, queries, tests and confidence. VIVID generates dialogues, answers and rubrics for instructors to compare and edit; it evaluates authored material without a learner test.
  18. Two paired empirical comparisons show why outcomes must be kept separate. In the decision task, no AI, explanation, and cognitive forcing yield 17, 35, and 33 percent fully correct decisions overall, but 18, 3, and 9 percent on items with incorrect AI advice. In the practice task, QuizBot and flashcards yield 87.2 and 65.8 percent recognition and require 46 and 14.3 minutes for equal repetitions. These are different studies and outcomes, not a pooled effect.
  19. Two schematic designs address attention switching through linked highlighting or integration. A separate interpretive comparison asks when each arrangement helps, drawing on evidence about correspondence guidance and representation type.
  20. A coverage panel accounts for all 4,331 Stream 1 papers: 696 have a recorded link to Stream 2, 2,477 have reference data but no observed link, and 1,158 lack reference data. A ranked bar chart shows the seven Stream 2 works cited by the most distinct Stream 1 papers.
  21. Learning is measured in 301 of 3,016 eligible papers. Directions are gain in 262, null in 36, loss in one, and unresolved in two. The loss code denotes smaller immediate gains than a comparator, not demonstrated decline below baseline. Duration is single-session in 200, repeated sessions in 33, weeks in 39, deployed use in 24, and unclear in five.
  22. Learning-only papers, 160: responsive help 124, handover 24, reduced help five, and learning measured 92. Dual-intent papers, 489: 357, 47, four, and 142. Performance/sensemaking papers, 2,367: 1,685, 62, two, and 67.
  23. Three part-to-whole bars over the same 3,295 included cards. Claim scope against coded evidence: evidence supports the claim in 1,757 cards (53.3%) and the claim exceeds the evidence in 1,538 (46.7%). Over-reliance or de-skilling risk: not reported in 2,202 cards (66.8%), raised by authors in 889 (27.0%), measured by authors in 190 (5.8%), and noted by a reviewer in 14 (0.4%). Support decreases over time: no in 3,151 cards (95.6%), yes in 86 (2.6%), and unclear in 58 (1.8%).
  24. A four-by-five matrix of shares and counts with a reference row. The joint population has 3,160 papers. The capability-measured row has 296 papers: 81.8 percent carry guidance and feedback, compared with 71.1 percent overall; 41.9 percent carry user control and expression, compared with 50.1 percent overall. The self-reported learning row has 145 papers, and the no-measurement row has 1,551.
  25. Learning was measured in 338 of 3,295 papers. Of 336 papers with reported results, 292 report gains, 43 null results, and one a loss. Of 332 with specified duration, 225 use a single session, 38 repeated sessions, 43 observation over weeks, and 26 deployed use.
  26. Three intent groups separate contingency, responsibility transfer, fading, and measured learning. Learning-only papers, 185: 144, 25, 5, and 106 respectively. Dual-intent papers, 547: 395, 56, 5, and 157. Immediate performance or sensemaking papers, 2,563: 1,809, 68, 2, and 75. Within the 144 contingent learning-only designs, 122 code neither responsibility transfer nor fading. All 144 have resolved property fields. Among 338 measured-learning papers, 43 code responsibility transfer and four code fading, with two recording both. Another 292 code neither, and one has no property code.
  27. A five-by-four matrix of shares and counts with a reference row. In every row the first column, work the tool left to the person, holds about half. Work the tool created ranges from 19.1 percent for collaboration and perspective to 36.0 percent for user control and expression. Collaboration and perspective has the highest first-column share, about 57 percent. The last two columns, the whole loop retained and authority kept by design, hold about a tenth and under a tenth in every row.
  28. A grid of small line charts, one per Stream 1 open field, showing the share of each final vocabulary reached as more cards are read.
  29. Five line charts for distinct cited works, shared references, grounding kinds, positions, and relations, each scaled to its own final count.
  30. A grid of seven small line charts for support form, named constructs, affected activity and capability, and applicability conditions, contexts, and populations.
  31. Twenty-one horizontal bars locate the last new code within each open field. Two fields with only one batch provide no evidence about vocabulary growth across batches.

Tables

  1. Two columns align eight gallery rows with their recorded fields. Row zero identifies the paper and reading disposition. Rows one through seven record mechanisms; before-and-after work; delegated and retained activity and measures; claims and evidence; stated limitations; whole-paper codes; and codebook notes. Mechanism and evidence entries retain source passages and pages.
  2. Two columns align four gallery rows with their fields. Row zero identifies the paper and its position toward prior work. Row one records each cited source, its use, and page-grounded evidence. Row two identifies the pages read. Row three gives a synthesis note. The unit of the source row is a cited work within a focal paper.
  3. Two columns align three gallery rows with their fields. Row zero identifies the work and its overall position. Row one holds one to eight claims with question links, applicability, human work, outcome evidence, counterevidence, source excerpts, and uncertainty. Row two holds the page-grounded evidence store and links each excerpt to the claim it supports.
  4. Three cases are compared across four columns: case, permitted action, downstream assumptions, and what a check must distinguish. Interactive task decomposition distinguishes recovering an old version from carrying its manual edits into revised work. Plume distinguishes preserving wording from limiting its influence on further generation. LineageD distinguishes advancing from explicitly checking every pairing. These are source-grounded design comparisons, not effectiveness rankings.
  5. Three columns identify each dataset, its count and unit, and the analysis it supports. Separate rows distinguish selection accounting, historical closed and open coding, confirmed complete mappings, theory lineage, Stream 2 claims, and citation metadata. The rows prevent readers from interpreting card, paper, claim, observation, and reference-record counts as the same population.
  6. Seven rows compare control-coded papers with papers lacking that code, across historical, known-out-removed, and confirmed field-complete populations. The difference in tool-created work is 10.1, 10.3, and 11.5 percentage points respectively. In the confirmed population, instructing or feeding accounts for an 8.0-point difference and operating or coping for 3.6 points. These are coded descriptions, not measured workload or causal effects.
  7. Four columns give the dimension and value, definition, share, and number of papers. Three row groups describe work still required of the person, work retained by the person, and activity delegated to the tool. The values distinguish task work, tool-created obligations, judgment, checking, execution, social responsibility, and different delegated operations. Dagger-marked values indicate missing or unspecified evidence rather than a measured absence of work.
  8. Four columns give dimension and value, definition, share, and paper count. Row groups classify changes in workflow and the evidence used to assess learning. Workflow categories distinguish changed activity, automation, newly possible work, added work, and unchanged practice. Learning categories distinguish direct measurement, proxies, self-report, and no measurement; frequencies do not estimate effectiveness.
  9. Five columns list 110 mechanism facets grouped into five families and eighteen categories. Each row gives a definition, up to three recorded values with citations beside the value illustrated, the number of distinct papers carrying the facet, and activity numbers. Activities are specifying intent, representing possibilities, evaluating alternatives, acting and revising, and memory and coordination. Counts can overlap across facets and activities.
  10. Five rows compare mechanism-family presence across three AI approaches. Columns report the chi-square statistic, adjusted probability, and Cramer association size. Association sizes range from 0.034 for explanation and visibility to 0.104 for collaboration and perspective. Differences describe family prevalence rather than performance effects.

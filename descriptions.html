Figure and table descriptions

Text descriptions accompany the figures and tables in the current paper. Follow a numbered link to read the figure or table in context.

Figures

  1. Figure 1 · Three aligned panels introduce two research questions, a dual-stream review, and the findings. The questions ask how AI tools redistribute human work and how scaffold designs support work and learning. Design corpus shows mechanism-card and theory-lineage-card counts for selected AI papers. Prior studies shows claim-card and claim counts for retained sources. The two streams converge on comparison with prior evidence. The findings concern workflows and cognitive work, and mechanisms that support work and learning: five families and eighteen categories.

  2. Figure 2 · Design corpus applies the same AI involvement, task meaning and human use criteria at screening and full text, then branches into mechanism and theory-lineage cards. Prior studies assesses prior theoretical and empirical claims. Both bodies of work contribute to comparisons of what people practice and learn.

  3. Figure 3 · Stacked bars count selected records by publication year in seven numbered venue groups. Year totals for 2019 through 2026 are 600, 692, 756, 776, 865, 987, 1,457, and 1,239; the 2026 bar covers papers published by 16 July. Human-computer interaction is the largest group in every year, and unavailable proceedings explain zero selections for some groups. Below the chart, each of the forty venues has its selection count and share.

  4. Figure 4 · Design corpus applies three criteria together: AI involvement, task meaning in an implemented or studied structure, and human inspection or action through that structure. Prior studies assesses claims and their supporting passages.

  5. Figure 5 · Twelve activity groups pair zero-based paper-count bars with annual percentage lines, separately for blue traditional AI/ML and orange GenAI. All rows share a zero-to-60-percent scale. The lower bars show annual coding denominators. Counts refer to distinct papers with activity codes; partial 2026 lines are dashed.

  6. Figure 6 · Twelve small interface scenes arranged in a four-column grid, each named with its activity group from the workflow coding. Orange identifies the relevant structure or action.

  7. Figure 7 · Two ranked bar charts show work delegated to tools and retained by people. Evaluating and classifying leads the tool panel; deciding and authorizing leads the human panel. Both use a common paper-count scale with separate coding denominators for percentages. Learning and reflecting is separate from supervising the system; allocation conditions and unspecified work are listed with the chart data.

  8. Figure 8 · Two columns show small interface scenes and their coding distinctions. Tool work spans perception, search and memory; representation and translation; computation and judgement; content generation; and decision, guidance and action. Retained human work spans judgement and decision; doing and revising; checking outputs; and responsibility, social and self-directed work.

  9. Figure 9 · Two columns of nine illustrated relation cards. In every card, represented task meaning appears on the left and possible human participation on the right, connected through a distinct interface schematic. The eighteen categories cover system behavior, evidence, summaries, sensory form, structure, comparison, retrieval, memory, intent, editing, candidates, rehearsal, guidance, feedback, help, workflow, reflection, and shared perspectives. Orange sidebars mark sensory form, instruction and pacing, and shared perspectives because these categories combine operations with conditions that can vary across the other relations. The original five-family hierarchy does not determine the explanatory layout.

  10. Figure 10 · A forest plot of all five mechanism families. The horizontal axis is GenAI minus traditional AI/ML prevalence, in percentage points. The zero line means no adjusted difference. User control is positively associated with GenAI and organization and comparison negatively associated in the primary model. Intervals for the other families cross zero. P-values use Holm correction across 51 contrasts. Two bars show the numbers of coded papers by technology.

  11. Figure 11 · Average annual change in each of the five mechanism families. Filled charcoal points show 2019 to 2025; hollow teal points show the recent 2023 to 2025 window. Lines show 95 percent confidence intervals. Collaboration and perspective increases in both windows. Guidance and feedback increases in the primary window, with less precise recent evidence. Separate columns show Holm-adjusted temporal p-values.

  12. Figure 12 · Four overlapping circles show specifying intent, representing possibilities, evaluating alternatives, and acting and revising. A return arrow connects activities, above a shared layer of memory and coordination through annotations, histories and shared views.

  13. Figure 13 · Two views of a document containing the phrase Group A and three bars. The author view marks a proposed link with a dotted underline and offers Accept and Discard. The reader view shows an accepted link; a cursor over the phrase highlights bar A and fades bars B and C.

  14. Figure 14 · Five ranked bars compare sources by the number of papers that use them, split by whether the citing design takes something concrete from the source. A chronological diagram follows the highest-ranked dated source into later designs.
